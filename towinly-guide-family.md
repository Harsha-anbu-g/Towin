# Towinly — the whole idea, and every feature explained

This guide is written for someone seeing Towinly for the first time. It explains what the app is, why it exists, every feature it has, and how to use each one. Three real people from the app's live demo appear throughout as examples: Margaret, an elder; Harsha, a helper; and Sarah, Margaret's daughter. You can log in as any of them at [www.towinly.com](https://www.towinly.com) and see everything in this guide for yourself.

---

## 1. The idea in one minute

Towinly is a community that brings older people ("Elders") together with younger people ("Helpers") for friendship, company, and a hand with everyday tasks.

The problem it solves is simple. Small daily things — shopping, a ride, someone to talk to — take energy that elders don't always have. And even when help is nearby, letting a stranger into your life is a big step. Help is hard to find alone, and trust is earned, not given.

Towinly's answer is a two-sided exchange:

|         | They have                              | They need                    |
| ------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Elders  | Time, money, and life lessons to share | Energy and company           |
| Helpers | Energy, time, and good company         | Money, care, and life advice |

Towinly is where they meet and share — and both win. That is the motto, written exactly this way everywhere in the app: _It takes two To Win._

And there is a third person in the picture: family. An elder can invite up to five of their own family members into the app to watch over these new friendships — to see they checked in today, follow how each friendship is growing, and step in when asked. Always on the elder's terms: one switch shares or hides each friendship, and the elder can flip it at any time. Help for the elder, peace of mind for the family.

The whole product is built around one mechanic: trust that grows slowly, one small safe step at a time. The brand is a tortoise — slow and steady, never rushed. Nothing personal is shared until trust has grown, both people must agree to every step, and no one is ever stuck.

Towinly is free. There is no charge to sign up or to use anything in the app.

## 2. Meet the three kinds of people

Everyone on Towinly is one of three kinds of people. You choose which one you are when you sign up.

An Elder is an older person looking for friendship, company, or help with daily tasks. In our story this is Margaret, 72, a retired teacher in Montreal who loves chess, gardening, and a good cup of tea.

A Helper is a younger person who gives time, company, and a hand with everyday things — and earns thanks, money, and a growing reputation for it. (Any money is something the two people arrange between themselves; Towinly moves no payments and takes no cut.) In our story this is Harsha, 23, who loves chess and can fix anything with a screen. He is Margaret's newest friend on the app.

A Family member is a relative who stays close: they can see how their elder's friendships are growing, hear right away if something needs attention, and help out when asked. In our story this is Sarah, Margaret's daughter. She doesn't need help herself and doesn't offer help to strangers — she is on Towinly purely to watch over her mum.

These three meet in one simple loop: Margaret asks for help or makes a friend, Harsha answers, the two of them climb a trust ladder together step by step, and Sarah — because Margaret chose to share that friendship with her — can watch the whole journey and lend a hand.

## 3. How trust works — the heart of the app

Two things make Towinly different from an ordinary social app: the Trust Journey and the Trust Score. Everything else in the app hangs off these two.

### The Trust Journey (the trust ladder)

Every friendship on Towinly grows through the same seven stages, like a tree growing roots:

| Stage | Name           | What it unlocks                                             |
| ----- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Just Connected | You can see each other's profiles                           |
| 2     | Messaging      | Private chat opens                                          |
| 3     | Phone Ready    | Phone numbers are shared                                    |
| 4     | Video Ready    | You agree you're ready for video calls                      |
| 5     | Social Media   | You swap social pages                                       |
| 6     | Ready to Meet  | Meeting in person — the elder's emergency contacts are told |
| 7     | Fully Trusted  | Reviews unlock; the friendship is complete                  |

The rules of the ladder:

- Both people must agree to every step. Moving up is a two-part handshake: the elder always goes first ("Advance"), then the helper accepts on their side. One person alone can never push a friendship forward.
- Nothing personal is shared early. A phone number only appears once a pair reaches Phone Ready — the app itself keeps it hidden before that.
- Either person can end a friendship at any time — one tap on the friend's card, with a gentle confirm step. No one is ever stuck.
- Each step reached earns each person one trust point with that friend.

In the demo you can see the ladder at every stage. Margaret and Harsha are at stage 6, Ready to Meet — they are planning their first in-person meeting, chess at the community centre on Saturday. Margaret and Priya (another helper) are at Phone Ready, one press away from the next stage — log in as Margaret and a live confirmation is waiting for you. And Grace, another elder, has walked the entire ladder with Harsha: their chat history reads like a little story, from a tablet "full of buttons and no idea which one rings" all the way to a first meeting in a busy park with lemon cake.

### The Trust Score

Every member has a visible Trust Score, and elders can see a helper's score before they ever say yes to them. The score is earned through real relationships, not form-filling: each person you build a friendship with counts for up to 15 points.

- Up to 7 points for the trust ladder — one per stage you reach together.
- Up to 5 points from that person's review of you — one per star.
- Plus your own profile points, counted again with every friendship. Helpers earn up to 3 (photo and bio, extra details, and a verified phone and ID); elders earn up to 2, plus 1 more for having family connected on the app.

Your profile points also count once on their own, before any friendships — so a finished profile gives you your very first points, and every friendship after that carries them again. Even so, a helper with three fully-trusted, five-star friendships has a far higher score than someone who just filled in a nice profile — which is exactly the point.

Scores add up into tiers: New Member (0), Getting Started (1+), Reliable (15+), Highly Trusted (45+), and Community Champion (90+). Your score is shown as a warm gold seal on your Trust Score page, along with a friendly breakdown of exactly where every point came from and what to do next.

## 4. Getting started

### Creating an account

On the sign-up page you first answer one question: who are you joining as? Elder, Helper, or "I'm here for a family member" (which creates a Family account — you link to your parent inside the app afterwards). Then a username, an email, and a password — and a checkbox for the Terms and Privacy pages, which spell out the rules and what happens to your information. Signing up with Google works too and is the fastest way in: your email is already confirmed by Google, so your account is created right away.

With a normal sign-up, Towinly confirms your email before your account even exists: you get a link by email, and clicking it is what creates the account. This means everyone you meet on Towinly has a confirmed, real email address.

Once you're in, your Profile page (under your name in the top bar) is where you introduce yourself: a photo, a few lines about you, your interests or skills, your town. It is also where you verify your phone number, upload an ID for a human to check, and — for elders — keep emergency contacts. A fuller profile earns trust points and makes your card warmer to a stranger deciding whether to say yes.

### Logging in

You can log in with your username, email, or phone number (if you've added one to your profile) — whichever you remember. There is a "Forgot password?" link, and repeated wrong guesses are blocked for a while to keep accounts safe. And if you've been away a long time, Towinly signs you out for safety and says so on the login page — just log in again.

### Trying it without an account

The login page has a demo box: "Just want to see how it works?" Three buttons log you straight into a fully-lived-in sample account:

- Try as an Elder — you become Margaret.
- Try as a Helper — you become Harsha.
- Try as Family — you become Sarah, Margaret's daughter.

You can click anything, send messages, accept requests, climb ladders — and about five minutes after you stop, the demo world quietly resets itself to the way it was, ready for the next visitor.

## 5. The daily check-in and streaks

For elders and helpers, the first screen after logging in is not a feed — it is a quiet check-in page. One big blue button: "I'm here today." (Family members land on their own "My Parents" screen instead — that story comes later.)

Tapping it once a day builds a streak: a count of days in a row, a week tracker with little check marks, and your best streak ever. A small card even shows how many days you have been alive, if you've added your date of birth — a gentle reminder that every day counts.

The check-in matters for a reason beyond the streak: it is how family knows you are okay. When Margaret taps "I'm here today," Sarah's screen shows a green band that says "All looks well — Margaret checked in today." If Margaret goes quiet for several days, Sarah sees that too (more on this in the Family section).

After checking in you can continue to your dashboard — or play Peekaboo, a small pair-matching memory game built into the app as one more nice reason to come back tomorrow. In the demo, Margaret has a 7-day streak going (her best is 14).

## 6. Finding each other

There are two ways strangers meet on Towinly, and both live right on the dashboard.

### Making friends directly

Both elders and helpers have an "Add Friends" tab. It shows people near you — the app asks (politely, and only if you want) to use your location, or you can simply type your town. A distance bar lets you widen the circle: 5, 10, 25, 50, or 100 km.

Each card shows the person's photo, age, city and distance, a line about them, their interests or skills — and, importantly, their gold trust chip: score and tier. Margaret can see that a helper is "Reliable · 38 points" before doing anything at all. Tap a name or photo and their full profile opens — their story, their verified badges, and every review other members have left them.

Tap "Add Friend" and they get an invite in their own Add Friends tab, with Accept and Decline buttons. Accepting usually starts the friendship at stage 1, Just Connected, and the trust ladder appears on both dashboards. Reliability already earned travels with you, though: when the person who sent the invite has a high Trust Score, the new friendship begins further up the ladder — at Phone Ready from 51 points, at Social Media from 71 — a head start their track record has paid for. In the demo, Nina has sent Margaret an invite that's waiting for an answer, and Margaret has one out to Sofia.

### Meeting through a help request

The other path is need first, friendship after: an elder posts a request for help, a nearby helper answers it, and accepting them starts a connection automatically. That path is the next section.

## 7. Help requests

### For elders: asking for help

The elder dashboard has a "Post Help" tab. The form is deliberately simple:

1. What do you need help with? — e.g. "Weekly grocery shopping."
2. A few details in your own words.
3. What kind of help — Company, Rides, Shopping, Cleaning, or Other.
4. How urgent — Normal or Urgent.

If location is on, nearby helpers are matched first; if not, the request still reaches everyone. Posted requests live in the "Posted Help" tab under three headings: Looking for Help, In Progress, and Completed.

When helpers offer, the elder sees every applicant with their name and message, and picks one. Accepting does three things at once: that helper is chosen, the others are politely turned down, and a friendship between the two is started (or an existing one is used). When the help is done, the elder taps "Mark as Complete."

Margaret's demo account shows the whole range: a morning-walk companion request that's still open, an urgent ride to a doctor's appointment with an offer from Nina waiting, weekly grocery shopping already assigned to Priya, a completed tablet set-up, and one cancelled request.

### For helpers: offering help

Helpers see the mirror image in their "Offer Help" tab: requests from elders nearby, sorted by distance, with the elder's name and when it was posted. One tap — "Offer to Help" — and the offer is in. While the elder decides, the card says exactly what's happening: "Waiting to hear back. The elder reviews all helpers and picks one." You can withdraw an offer any time before it's answered.

In the demo, David (76, retired engineer) has posted "Weekly chess and tea company" — and both Priya and Harsha have offered. Harsha's message: "fair warning, I play to win!"

### Reviews close the loop

Once a pair reaches Fully Trusted, they can review each other — whether the friendship began with a help request or a simple friend invite: one to five stars, kind word tags (Friendly, Punctual, Respectful, Helpful, Patient), and an optional comment. Reviews are deliberately locked until Fully Trusted — finishing one job is not a shortcut to a good reputation; a real relationship is. Each star becomes a trust point for the person reviewed.

A review can also carry a quiet safety flag. Ticking "Flag a safety concern" sends it to the Towinly team and keeps the reviewer anonymous.

In the demo, Grace has left Harsha five stars: "Harsha is patient, knowledgeable, and genuinely kind." Margaret hasn't reviewed anyone yet — on purpose, so that when you log in as her, the Review button is live on the cards of Tom and Claire, two helpers she has already walked the full ladder with, and you can press it yourself.

## 8. Messages

Chat opens at stage 2 of the trust ladder, Messaging — before that, the chat screen shows a friendly notice that both of you need to confirm the step on your dashboards first.

The inbox groups conversations by who they're with — Elders, Helpers, Family, and Groups — with unread badges and a search box. Each conversation row shows the friendship's current trust stage as a small pill (family chats deliberately show no stage — family is family, not a ladder to climb).

Inside a chat it works the way you'd hope: bubbles, date lines, "Seen," and a simple box to type in. Above the messages sits a one-line hint matched to the stage you're at — "Ready for a phone call? Share your number when comfortable," or at stage 6, "Planning your first meet? Choose a public place." A Report button sits in the corner of every chat, always one tap away.

Some chats have three or more voices. Every shared friendship gets a "Family updates" notes thread — Margaret, Harsha, and Sarah all read and write the same notes there (that thread opens for family once the friendship reaches Ready to Meet). In the demo it holds a small story: Harsha fixed the wifi over a video call, Sarah thanked him ("That router has been defeating us for months!"), and Margaret confirmed Saturday's chess game.

## 9. Family — the part that makes Towinly different

Most apps connect two people. Towinly's family feature adds the third person who, in real life, was always there: the daughter who worries, the son who lives far away. It is built on one principle repeated all over the app: the elder is always in charge.

### Linking your family

An elder can link up to five family members. Either side can start it — Margaret can add Sarah from her "My Family" page, or Sarah can add her mum from her own "Add Parent" tab. You type the person's exact Towinly username, email, or phone, and what they are to you ("Daughter, Son, Niece…"). Nothing happens until the other person says yes — every link is a two-sided consent.

Once linked, Margaret sees Sarah in her family list and can mark her as the main contact. Removing a family member takes one tap and is honest about what it means: they will no longer see that you're safe, or any friendship you shared. Removing someone also wipes every permission they ever had — re-linking later starts from zero, with fresh consent.

Having family connected earns an elder one flat trust point — one point total, whether one family member or five.

### The sharing switch — one switch that controls everything

Each friendship card on Margaret's dashboard has a switch: "Share with family — let them see this friendship." Every friendship starts private. Only the elder can flip the switch, and it works per friendship — Margaret has shared only her friendship with Harsha; her friendships with Tom, Claire, and Priya are hers alone, and Sarah cannot see they exist.

Turning sharing on gives the family exactly four things, and the app promises the same four things on both sides of the switch, phrased for each side:

1. See how the friendship is going, step by step — they cannot change anything.
2. Follow the small updates thread on it, together with the elder and the helper (this thread opens once the friendship reaches Ready to Meet).
3. Talk to that helper too, once the elder and helper are messaging.
4. Use anything the elder allows on the "Act for me" tab — those permissions only work on shared friendships.

Turning the switch off takes all four away instantly. Nothing is remembered or cached — a friendship made private disappears from the family's view at that moment.

### What Sarah sees — the family's side

Sarah's home screen ("My Parents") answers the only question that really matters, before anything else: is Mum okay? Each parent card leads with a reassurance band — green "All looks well — Margaret checked in today," grey if there's no check-in yet, gold "It's been quiet" if several days have passed, red if Margaret has pressed her SOS button.

Opening Margaret's page, Sarah sees:

- Friendships shared with her — for each one, the helper's profile and gold trust badge, the same seven-stage trust ladder Margaret sees (read-only), and a note when a first in-person meeting is coming up.
- A direct line to the helper. This is Towinly's trust inheritance: once Margaret and Harsha are at Messaging or beyond on a shared friendship, Sarah can message Harsha directly — no request, no waiting. She stands behind her mother's earned trust. Sarah's chat with Harsha in the demo says it well: "Hi Harsha! Mum says you're the chess player. Thank you for the wifi rescue." If Margaret ever stops sharing the friendship, that chat closes by itself — and Sarah can also pause it from her own side at any time, and resume it later.
- The shared updates thread for that friendship.
- How Margaret is today — her check-in status and her open help requests.
- News — a tab of family alerts. There are exactly three kinds: urgent help (Margaret pressed SOS), quiet lately (no check-in for days), and first meeting (a shared friendship reached Ready to Meet). Alerts appear in the app only — Towinly never texts or emails someone just for being family, so the elder's life never becomes a stream of notifications about them. (Text messages only ever go to the people an elder has listed as emergency contacts — Sarah happens to be both.)

On the helper's side, Harsha sees the mirror image: under Margaret's card on his dashboard, a small row — "Sarah — Margaret's daughter" — with a Message button and the note that the two of them can talk while the friendship stays shared. He always knows exactly who stands behind the elder he is helping, and never more than that.

And Margaret sees everything: her family page shows her every helper each family member can reach and every chat they've opened. Nothing that family can see or do is ever hidden from the elder.

### Act for me — helping hands, with consent

Some elders want family to do more than watch. "Act for me" lets Margaret hand Sarah up to three specific abilities, each explained on the switch in plain words:

- Ask for help for you — Sarah can post and close help requests on Margaret's behalf. Helpers always see it was Sarah asking: the request reads "Asked by Sarah, for Margaret."
- Move a friendship forward for you — Sarah can take Margaret's next step on the trust ladder. The step still counts as Margaret's, and the helper sees Sarah took it for her.
- Leave a review for you — Sarah can rate a fully-trusted helper. The review is Margaret's, with Sarah's name on it as the person who wrote it.

The rules are strict and simple. Everything starts off. Only the elder can turn a permission on. Sarah can ask for one — which places a yes/no card on Margaret's page, in the exact same words the switch uses — but asking never grants anything, and if Margaret says "not now," Sarah simply sees "you can ask again in a few days," never a hurtful "declined." Every action Sarah takes is signed with her name — in chat threads it literally reads "Sarah, writing for Margaret" — so nothing done for an elder ever pretends to be done by them. And the two friendship abilities — moving a step forward and leaving a review — only work on friendships Margaret has shared: watching gates doing. Asking for help is the one that works for Margaret as a whole, because a help request belongs to her, not to any single friendship.

In the demo, this is live mid-story: Margaret has given Sarah the first two permissions, Sarah has posted "A lift to Mum's hearing test" on her behalf, and Sarah's request for the third — leaving reviews — is sitting on Margaret's page, waiting for her answer. Log in as Margaret and the decision is yours.

## 10. The safety net

Safety features run under everything, and most of them you hopefully never see:

- SOS button. Elders have a red SOS button in the top bar of every page. Pressing it starts a short countdown (tap again to cancel), then sends an urgent text message to all of the elder's emergency contacts and an alert to their linked family.
- Emergency contacts. Every elder can save up to three, with name, phone, and relationship — they don't need to be on Towinly at all. Margaret's is Sarah.
- First-meeting notice. When a friendship reaches Ready to Meet, the elder's emergency contacts are told automatically. First meetings never happen in silence.
- Quiet-days watch. If an elder hasn't used the app for five days, their emergency contacts get a text and their family gets a "quiet lately" alert. A missed streak becomes a phone call from someone who loves you.

  (The in-app family alerts always arrive the instant these things happen. Anything sent as a text message — including phone-verification codes — goes out where the service's text line is switched on.)

- Verification. Members can verify their phone number with a code and upload an ID document that a human reviews. Verified badges appear on profiles, and both feed the trust score.
- Reporting. Every chat and profile has a report option; a safety concern in a review is anonymous.
- Quiet protections. Confirmed emails before an account exists, limits on repeated login attempts and requests, and no personal contact details ever crossing to anyone before the ladder stage that unlocks them.
- Your data is yours. You can ask for a full copy of everything Towinly holds about you, or for your account to be deleted completely — photos, messages, and all. The Privacy page explains how to ask.

## 11. Ask AI — the tortoise helper

A small "Ask AI" button floats on nearly every page. It opens a chat with the Towinly tortoise, a helper that answers questions about how the app works: "How does the Trust Journey work?", "What is my trust score?", "What should I do today?"

Logged in, it knows your own account — your score, your streak, your unfinished profile items — and gives friendly, personal suggestions ("Add a photo and you'll earn your next profile point"). It only ever sees your own information, never other people's or your messages.

It was built for elders' hands and eyes: you can speak your question aloud instead of typing, and every answer has a "Read aloud" button.

## 12. Built elder-first

Towinly's look and feel is a feature in itself, and every screen follows the same rules:

- One idea per screen. No feeds, no clutter, nothing urgent blinking for attention.
- Big and readable: text never smaller than comfortable book print, buttons big enough to tap easily with a thumb, strong contrast, on a warm paper-like background — never a dark screen unless you flip the Night mode switch in your account menu yourself.
- Calm colours with meaning: sky-blue is always the action to take, gold always means trust, green always means achieved.
- Plain, everyday words everywhere. Waiting screens always say what you're waiting for and who decides — "Waiting for Margaret to accept — only they can say yes."
- Gentle motion, and less of it if your device asks for reduced motion.

There is also a public "How It Works" guided tour with tabs for each role — I'm an Elder, I'm a Helper, I'm Family — eight short steps each, and a feedback button on every page that goes straight to the team.
